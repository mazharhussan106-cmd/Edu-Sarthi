# Nayi language seekhte waqt pehle kaunse chunks seekhein?

*Ek student ki nazar se, research ke aadhar par*

Ek honest baat pehle: main (Claude) language waise nahi seekhta jaise insaan seekhta hai, isliye "main student hota to" wala jawab apni pasand se nahi, research ke hisaab se diya gaya hai. Maan kar chala gaya hai ki target language ka ek bhi word nahi aata aur goal hai jaldi bolna shuru karna.

---

## 1. Chunks itne important kyun hain

- Sabse zyada quote hone wali study **Erman & Warren (2000)** ki hai. Unke hisaab se spoken English ka lagbhag **58.6%** aur written English ka **52.3%** formulaic sequences (pehle se bane word combinations) se bana hota hai.
- Yeh figure definition par depend karta hai. Alag researchers ne **17–30% se lekar 80%** tak ke figures diye hain. Zyada cautious estimate: language ka **ek-tihaai se aadha** hissa formulaic hota hai (Conklin & Schmitt; Schmitt 2010).
- Consistent pattern: **spoken conversation sabse zyada formulaic** hoti hai, newspaper language sabse kam (Vilkaitė).

**Student ke liye matlab:** agar goal bolna hai, to chunks sabse bada shortcut hain.

---

## 2. Priority ranking: kis order mein seekhein

### Level 1 — "Seekhne mein madad karne wale" survival chunks

Examples: *Sorry, can you say that again? · What does ___ mean? · How do you say ___? · I don't understand · Slowly, please* — aur greetings, thank you, sorry.

- Nattinger & DeCarrico isse **"social interaction"** category kehte hain.
- Number 1 par kyun: yeh chunks har conversation ko ek class bana dete hain.
- Research: French seekhne wale beginners shuru mein formulaic sequences par bahut depend karte the, kyunki apne grammar se woh classroom ki baat-cheet nahi chala sakte the (Myles, Hooper & Mitchell).

### Level 2 — Slot wale sentence stems (sabse zyada ROI)

Examples: *My name is ___ · I'm from ___ · I want to ___ · Can I ___? · I like ___ · Do you ___?*

- Yeh **"necessary topic"** markers hain — roz ki baat-cheet ke common topics.
- Yeh aadha grammar khud saath laate hain.
- Research: Myles, Hooper & Mitchell (1998) ne 16 beginner learners (11–13 saal) ko 2 saal track kiya. *j'aime* (I like), *j'habite* (I live) jaise phrases fixed expressions ki tarah sikhaye gaye the. Zyada tar learners ne dheere-dheere in chunks ko **"unpack"** kiya aur unke parts se naye sentences banaye. Rote learning aur rule-building ek doosre ko feed karte hain.
- **Stem ratna cheating nahi hai — yahi grammar tak pahunchne ka raasta hai.**

### Level 3 — Sabse frequent spoken collocations aur fillers

Examples: *you know · I think · a bit · a lot of · at the moment · thank you · come back · have a look*

- Shin & Nation (2008) ne 10 million spoken words analyse kiye. **84 collocations** itni frequent nikli ki woh top 1,000 words ke level mein aa sakti hain, aur **308** top 2,000 mein.
- Unka conclusion: yeh collocations bhi utna hi attention deserve karti hain jitna high-frequency single words ko milta hai.
- Scale: *you know* spoken corpus mein 27,348 baar aaya, jabki sabse frequent written collocation *of course* sirf 2,698 baar.
- Isi group mein **time-buying chunks** bhi: *let me think · how can I say* — taaki sochte waqt chup na hona pade.

### Level 4 — Connectors aur discourse devices

Examples: *because · so · but · for example · as a result*

- Yeh phrases discourse ke meaning aur structure ko jodte hain.
- Stems aa jaane ke baad, inse chhote sentences ko jodna seekhein.

### Level 5 — Idioms aur opaque phrases (sabse last)

Examples: *break the ice · at the end of the day* · phrasal verbs ke tricky meanings

- Martinez & Schmitt (2012) ki **PHRASE List** mein aise 505 expressions hain, lekin woh list mainly **receptive use** (samajhne) ke liye hai, bolne ke liye nahi.
- **Trap:** yeh expressions lagbhag poori tarah top 2,000 words se bane hain, isliye learner ko lagta hai ki samajh aa gaya, jabki meaning alag hota hai.
- Inhe tab padhein jab sunkar ya padhkar confusion hone lage — shuru mein nahi.

---

## 3. Seekhein kaise (sirf list ratna kaafi nahi)

- **Noticing kaam karta hai:** Boers et al. (2006) mein jis group ko authentic material mein word combinations notice karna sikhaya gaya, usse blind judges ne interview mein zyada proficient mana. Formulaic sequences ki ginti oral proficiency ratings ke saath correlate hui.
- **Lekin noticing kaafi nahi:** wahi authors kehte hain ki sirf "phrase noticing" se aage badhkar active phrase learning zaroori hai.
- **Practical plan:**
  1. Chunk ko ek real sentence mein suno
  2. Zor se bolo
  3. Apne baare mein ek sentence banao (*"I want to learn Japanese"*)
  4. Spaced repetition se dohraao

### Ek limit

Zyada inflection wali languages mein effect kam ho sakta hai. English aur Spanish ki study (Stengers et al. 2011) mein correlation English mein sabse strong tha; Spanish mein inflection errors ki wajah se chunks ka faayda kam dikha. Yaani Hindi, Russian ya Arabic jaisi languages mein **stems ke saath basic grammar endings bhi jaldi seekhne padenge.**

---

## 4. Ek line ka jawab

> **Survival chunks → slot wale sentence stems → top spoken collocations aur fillers → connectors → idioms (sabse last).**

---

## 5. Speak Sarthi ke liye takeaway

Speak Sarthi ke 7,053 chunks ko agar is order mein tag/sort kiya jaye (survival → stems → frequent collocations → connectors → idioms), to beginner ko pehle din se hi **bolne layak** cheezein milengi, sirf padhne layak nahi.
