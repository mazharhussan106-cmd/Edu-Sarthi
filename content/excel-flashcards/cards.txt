level: Beginner
source: Claude knowledge
term: Cells, ranges and cell references
cue: How does Excel name one box on the sheet, and how do you point to a whole block of boxes in a formula?
pronunciation: cell = सेल | range = रेंज | reference = रेफ़रेंस
kind: Core concept
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: A worksheet is a grid, so each box needs an address; the column letter plus row number, like C3, works like a map coordinate.
pro_tip: Click a cell and look at the Name Box on the left of the formula bar to see its exact address.
typing_hint: Write the column letter first, then the row number: C3, not 3C. A colon joins two corners of a range.
memory_story: Think of a cinema seat: Row 3, Seat C is C3. A range A1:B2 is a small group of friends booking four seats together.
visual: A chessboard where one square glows with the label C3 and a rectangle of four squares is outlined in green.
emotion: The calm feeling of finally knowing exactly where every number lives.
meaning: A cell is one box on the sheet, and a range is a block of cells.
native_meaning: सेल = शीट का एक खाना; रेंज = कई खानों का समूह
code: A1: 10¶A2: 20¶A3: 30¶B1: =SUM(A1:A3)
output: 60
explanation: A cell address is the column letter followed by the row number. A range uses a colon between its first and last cell, so A1:A3 means A1, A2 and A3. Formulas use these addresses so the answer updates when the numbers change.
simple_explain: Har khane ka ek address hota hai, jaise A1. Kai khane ek saath lene hon to colon lagate hain, jaise A1:A3. Formula me address likhne se number badalne par answer khud badal jata hai.
analogy: Like a cinema seat number: row and seat together tell you exactly one seat, and a seat block is a group of neighbouring seats.
syntax: =FUNCTION(first_cell:last_cell)
anatomy: A = column letter | 1 = row number | A1 = one cell | A1:A3 = range from A1 down to A3
example_1: A1: 10¶B1: =A1 (shows 10, B1 just points to one cell)
example_2: A1: 10¶A2: 20¶A3: 30¶B1: =SUM(A1:A3) (shows 60, a range down one column)
example_3: A1: 5¶A2: 10¶B1: 15¶B2: 20¶C1: =SUM(A1:B2) (shows 50, a block across two columns)
variation: =SUM(A1,A3) adds only A1 and A3 (comma picks separate cells)¶=SUM(A:A) adds the whole column A¶Name Box: type D10 and press Enter to jump to D10
used_with: SUM, AVERAGE, Name Box, formulas
related: Worksheet, Workbook, Named range, Table
alternatives: Named range, Excel Table column reference
word_family: cell, range, row, column, address, selection
where_used: Budgets and totals | Marks sheets | Any formula you write
mini_conversation: A: My total shows 0, what did I do wrong? | B: Check your range; you may have typed A1:A2 instead of A1:A3 and missed a row.
misconception: ✗ A1:A3 means only the two cells A1 and A3 → ✓ A1:A3 means every cell from A1 through A3, including A2
confused_with: A1:A3 vs A1,A3: the colon takes the whole block, the comma takes only the cells you name.
avoid: Do not type numbers into a formula when the cell address can be used, or the answer will not update.
wrong_code: A1: 10¶A2: 20¶A3: 30¶B1: =SUM(A1,A3)
right_code: A1: 10¶A2: 20¶A3: 30¶B1: =SUM(A1:A3)
memory_trick: Colon = continues through all cells; comma = counts only the ones named.
real_project: A school clerk sums the marks of one student by pointing a formula at the row range of that student.
real_interview: Interview question: What is the difference between a cell reference and a range, and which symbol makes a range?
real_daily: It is like telling a friend your flat number instead of saying the whole street, and a floor range instead of one flat.
mcq_question: A sheet has A1 = 4, A2 = 6, A3 = 8 and A4 = 10. What does =SUM(A2:A4) return?
mcq_options: A) 18 B) 24 C) 28 D) 30
mcq_answer: B
mcq_why: A2:A4 covers A2, A3 and A4, so 6 + 8 + 10 = 24; 28 would include A1 only by mistake, and 30 is a different total.
fill_blank: The address of the cell in column C and row 3 is ____ → C3
hindi_to_task: Write a formula that adds the numbers in cells B2 down to B6.
task_answer: B7: =SUM(B2:B6)
own_task: Type five numbers in column A and write a SUM formula for them in A6.
practice_question: A1: 4¶A2: 6¶A3: 8¶A4: 10¶B1: =SUM(A2:A4)¶What does B1 show?
practice_answer: 24

term: Data types: text, number, date
cue: Why can a cell that looks like a number refuse to add up, and what does Excel secretly store behind a date?
pronunciation: text = टेक्स्ट | number = नंबर | date = डेट
kind: Core concept
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: Excel must know whether a value is a word, an amount or a day, because each kind follows different maths rules.
pro_tip: If a number sits on the left of its cell, it is probably stored as text, so check it before you add it.
typing_hint: Typing an apostrophe before a number, like '100, makes Excel store it as text on purpose.
memory_story: Excel is a strict clerk with three boxes marked Words, Amounts and Days, and he drops every entry into one box without asking you.
visual: Three coloured trays on a desk, with a paper marked 100 sitting in the Words tray looking out of place.
emotion: Relief when a total that showed the wrong value finally starts adding correctly.
meaning: Excel stores each entry as text, a number or a date, and treats them differently.
native_meaning: टेक्स्ट = शब्द, नंबर = संख्या, डेट = तारीख
code: A1: 100¶A2: '100 (typed with an apostrophe, so it is text)¶B1: =ISNUMBER(A1)¶B2: =ISNUMBER(A2)
output: B1 shows TRUE and B2 shows FALSE
explanation: ISNUMBER checks if a cell holds a real number. A1 is a number, but A2 was stored as text because of the apostrophe, even though it looks the same. Dates are numbers too: Excel counts days from 1 January 1900, and 1 January 2024 is stored as 45292.
simple_explain: Excel teen cheez alag rakhta hai: text, number aur date. Text ke andar 100 likha ho to wo jodne me nahi aata. Date asal me ek number hai jo din ginta hai.
analogy: Like a bank counter with separate windows for cash, cheques and letters, each handled by different rules.
syntax: =ISNUMBER(cell)  |  =ISTEXT(cell)
anatomy: ISNUMBER = test function | cell = what to check | result = TRUE or FALSE
example_1: A1: Asha¶B1: =ISTEXT(A1) (shows TRUE, a word is text)
example_2: A1: 100¶A2: '100¶B1: =SUM(A1:A2) (shows 100, SUM skips the text one)
example_3: A1: =DATE(2024,1,1)¶A2: =DATE(2024,3,1)¶B1: =A2-A1 (cell format General, shows 60 days)
variation: Format cell as General to see the date serial number, for example 45292¶=VALUE(A2) converts text like "100" into the number 100¶Data tab > Text to Columns > Finish converts a column of text numbers
used_with: ISNUMBER, ISTEXT, VALUE, Format Cells
related: Number format, Text to Columns, Date serial number, VALUE
alternatives: VALUE function, Text to Columns, multiply by 1
word_family: text, number, date, time, logical, error value
where_used: Importing data from other systems | Cleaning mark lists | Date calculations like due dates
mini_conversation: A: My SUM ignores some numbers in the column, why? | B: They are probably stored as text; look for left-aligned values and convert them.
misconception: ✗ Anything that looks like a number is a number → ✓ A value can look like 100 but be stored as text, and then SUM ignores it
confused_with: Text 100 vs number 100: text looks the same but is ignored by SUM and sorts like a word.
avoid: Do not type numbers with extra spaces or apostrophes if you need to calculate with them.
wrong_code: A1: 100¶A2: '100¶A3: 50¶B1: =SUM(A1:A3) (expecting 250)
right_code: A1: 100¶A2: '100¶A3: 50¶B2: =VALUE(A2)¶B1: =SUM(A1,B2,A3) (shows 250)
memory_trick: Left = Letters (text), Right = Right numbers; this is the default alignment.
real_project: A sales analyst imports a CSV and converts text numbers so that totals match the source report.
real_interview: Interview question: Why does SUM give the wrong total when some numbers are stored as text?
real_daily: It is like a shopkeeper who counts only real notes and puts photocopies of notes aside.
mcq_question: A cell shows 2500 on the left side of the cell, and SUM leaves it out. What is the most likely reason?
mcq_options: A) It is stored as text B) It is formatted as currency C) It is stored as a date D) The column is too narrow
mcq_answer: A
mcq_why: Numbers align right by default and SUM skips text; currency or date formats do not make SUM skip a value.
fill_blank: Excel stores the date 1 January 2024 internally as the number ____ → 45292
hindi_to_task: Write a formula that tells whether cell A1 holds a number.
task_answer: B1: =ISNUMBER(A1)
own_task: Type a number and the same number with an apostrophe, then compare them with ISNUMBER.
practice_question: A1: 50¶A2: '50 (text)¶A3: 50¶B1: =SUM(A1:A3)¶What does B1 show?
practice_answer: 100

term: AutoFill and the fill handle
cue: Which tiny square at a cell's corner lets you drag a pattern or formula down a column?
pronunciation: AutoFill = ऑटोफ़िल | fill handle = फ़िल हैंडल
kind: Excel feature
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: Typing the same pattern a hundred times is slow, so Excel guesses the pattern and repeats it for you.
pro_tip: Double-click the fill handle to fill down as far as the data in the next column goes.
typing_hint: Select the cell, then grab the small square at its bottom-right corner; the pointer turns into a thin black plus.
memory_story: The fill handle is a tiny tap at the corner of a water tank; pull it down and the pattern pours into every cell below.
visual: A green square at the corner of a cell, being dragged down while numbers appear one after another.
emotion: A happy surprise when a whole column fills itself in one second.
meaning: AutoFill copies or continues a pattern by dragging the fill handle.
native_meaning: ऑटोफ़िल = खींचकर पैटर्न या फ़ॉर्मूला अपने आप भरना
code: A1: 1¶A2: 2¶Select A1:A2, drag the fill handle down to A5
output: A3 shows 3, A4 shows 4, A5 shows 5
explanation: Excel reads the first two values to find the step, here +1, and continues it. For text such as day names it continues the built-in list. For formulas it copies the formula and moves the relative references.
simple_explain: Do cell select karke handle kheenchoge to Excel pattern samajh kar aage bhar deta hai. Ek hi number ho to wo copy ho jata hai. Formula ho to wo bhi neeche copy hota hai.
analogy: Like showing a child 2, 4 and then saying carry on; the child continues 6, 8, 10 by the same rule.
syntax: Select cells > drag the fill handle (bottom-right square)
anatomy: Selected cells = the pattern | fill handle = small square | drag direction = where the pattern continues
example_1: A1: 1¶A2: 2¶Select A1:A2, drag the handle to A5 (A5 shows 5, a number series)
example_2: A1: Mon¶Drag the handle down to A3 (A2 shows Tue, A3 shows Wed, a text list)
example_3: A1: 5¶A2: 7¶A3: 9¶B1: =A1*2¶Double-click the handle of B1 (B2 shows 14, B3 shows 18, a formula copy)
variation: A single number such as 5 is copied, not counted, when you drag¶Hold Ctrl while dragging a single number to make it count up (Windows)¶Click the small Auto Fill Options button after dragging to choose Copy Cells or Fill Series
used_with: Fill series, relative references, Flash Fill, Ctrl+D
related: Relative reference, Custom lists, Ctrl+D, Flash Fill
alternatives: Ctrl+D to fill down, Home tab > Fill > Series
word_family: AutoFill, fill handle, fill series, fill down, fill right, series
where_used: Serial numbers on a list | Dates and months across columns | Copying one formula to every row
mini_conversation: A: I typed 1 and dragged but I got 1, 1, 1; why? | B: A single number is copied, so enter 1 and 2 first and drag both cells.
misconception: ✗ Dragging one number always counts up, 1, 2, 3 → ✓ A single number is copied; give two cells to show the step, or hold Ctrl while dragging
confused_with: Fill handle vs Ctrl+D: the handle drags to any distance and pattern, Ctrl+D copies the cell above into the selected cells.
avoid: Do not drag blindly over a long list when double-clicking the fill handle does the work in one move.
wrong_code: A1: 1¶Drag the handle down to A5 (expecting 1, 2, 3, 4, 5)
right_code: A1: 1¶A2: 2¶Select A1:A2 and drag the handle down to A5
memory_trick: Two cells show the step; one cell shows only copy.
real_project: An office assistant numbers 200 employee rows by dragging the handle instead of typing every number.
real_interview: Interview question: How do you quickly copy a formula down 500 rows without dragging?
real_daily: It is like a teacher writing the first two roll numbers and asking the class monitor to continue the list.
mcq_question: A1 contains 5 and nothing else. You drag the fill handle from A1 down to A3 without pressing any key. What does A3 show?
mcq_options: A) 3 B) 5 C) 7 D) 15
mcq_answer: B
mcq_why: One plain number is copied, so A2 and A3 both show 5; counting needs two example cells or the Ctrl key.
fill_blank: The small square at the bottom-right corner of a selected cell is called the ____ → fill handle
hindi_to_task: Fill the numbers 10, 20, 30, 40 in column A using only two typed cells.
task_answer: A1: 10¶A2: 20¶Select A1:A2 and drag the fill handle down to A4
own_task: Type Jan in one cell and drag it across to see the months continue.
practice_question: A1: 10¶A2: 20¶You select A1:A2 and drag the fill handle down to A4.¶What does A4 show?
practice_answer: 40

term: Relative vs absolute references ($)
cue: Why does a copied formula sometimes point to the wrong cell, and what sign pins a cell in place?
pronunciation: absolute = एब्सोल्यूट | relative = रिलेटिव | dollar sign = डॉलर साइन
kind: Core concept
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: Excel moves references when you copy a formula, and the dollar sign was added so you can lock a part that must not move.
pro_tip: Press F4 while the cursor is on a reference to cycle through A1, $A$1, A$1 and $A1 (on some laptops press Fn+F4).
typing_hint: The dollar sign goes before the part to lock: $B$1 locks column and row, B$1 locks only the row.
memory_story: A dollar sign is a pin that nails the cell to the board, so the formula cannot slide it when you copy it.
visual: A cell with a gold pin pushed through it while other cell references slide downward with the copied formulas.
emotion: A moment of relief when a whole column of answers turns out right after one tiny dollar sign.
meaning: A relative reference moves when copied; an absolute reference with dollar signs stays fixed.
native_meaning: रिलेटिव = कॉपी करने पर बदलता है; एब्सोल्यूट ($) = जगह पर टिका रहता है
code: A2: 200¶A3: 300¶B1: 0.1 (rate)¶C2: =A2*$B$1¶C3: =A3*$B$1
output: C2 shows 20 and C3 shows 30
explanation: When C2 is copied down, A2 becomes A3 because it is relative. $B$1 stays B1 because both its column and row are locked. This lets every row use the same rate cell.
simple_explain: Normal reference formula ke saath neeche khisakta hai. Dollar lagane se wo cell wahi rukta hai. Isliye rate wale cell par $ lagate hain.
analogy: Like asking each student to check the person next to them (relative), but also asking everyone to look at one fixed notice board (absolute).
syntax: A1 (relative) | $A$1 (absolute) | A$1 or $A1 (mixed)
anatomy: $ before the letter = lock the column | $ before the number = lock the row | no $ = free to move
example_1: A2: 200¶A3: 300¶C2: =A2*2 (copied to C3 it becomes =A3*2 and shows 600, relative)
example_2: A2: 200¶A3: 300¶B1: 0.1¶C2: =A2*$B$1 (copied to C3 it stays $B$1 and shows 30, absolute)
example_3: A2: 2¶A3: 3¶B1: 4¶C1: 5¶B2: =$A2*B$1 (shows 8; copied to C3 it becomes =$A3*C$1 and shows 15, mixed)
variation: F4 once makes $A$1, twice makes A$1, three times makes $A1, four times gives A1 again¶Named range, for example Rate, works like an absolute reference¶Copying a formula right changes the column letter; copying down changes the row number
used_with: AutoFill, SUM, VLOOKUP, percentage calculations
related: AutoFill, Named range, Mixed reference, F4 key
alternatives: Named range, Excel Table structured references
word_family: relative, absolute, mixed, anchor, lock, F4
where_used: Applying one tax or discount rate to a price list | Multiplication tables | Lookup tables that must not shift
mini_conversation: A: Only my first row is right after I drag the formula down; the rest show zero. | B: Your rate cell moved; press F4 to lock it as $B$1.
misconception: ✗ A copied formula always points to the same cells as the original → ✓ A normal reference shifts when copied; only the ones with $ stay fixed
confused_with: B1 vs $B$1: B1 shifts when copied, $B$1 never shifts.
avoid: Do not copy a formula that uses a fixed rate cell without first adding the dollar signs.
wrong_code: B1: 10¶A2: 200¶A3: 300¶C2: =A2*B1¶C3 (copied) becomes =A3*B2 and shows 0
right_code: B1: 10¶A2: 200¶A3: 300¶C2: =A2*$B$1¶C3 (copied) stays =A3*$B$1 and shows 3000
memory_trick: Dollar = Done moving; no dollar means the cell can wander.
real_project: A finance analyst applies one GST rate cell to hundreds of price rows using $ in the formula.
real_interview: Interview question: What does $A$1 mean, and when would you use it instead of A1?
real_daily: It is like fixing one price tag to a shelf so that it does not move while the other items change places.
mcq_question: C2 holds =A2*B1. You copy C2 down to C3. What does C3 contain?
mcq_options: A) =A3*B1 B) =A3*B2 C) =A2*B2 D) =A3*$B$1
mcq_answer: B
mcq_why: Without dollar signs both references move down by one row, so A2 becomes A3 and B1 becomes B2.
fill_blank: To lock both the column and row of B1 in a formula, write ____ → $B$1
hindi_to_task: Price is in A2:A4 and the discount rate is in D1. Write the formula for C2 so it can be copied down.
task_answer: C2: =A2*$D$1
own_task: Make a small price list and one tax cell, then copy a formula that uses the tax with a dollar sign.
practice_question: A1: 5¶B2: 10¶B3: 20¶C2: =B2*$A$1¶You copy C2 to C3.¶What does C3 show?
practice_answer: 100

topic: 02 Basic Functions
level: Beginner

term: SUM and AutoSum
cue: Which function adds a block of numbers, and what shortcut types it for you?
pronunciation: SUM = सम | AutoSum = ऑटो-सम
kind: Function
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: Adding is the most common job in a spreadsheet, so Excel gives a ready-made function and a button for it.
pro_tip: Select the cells below a column and press Alt+= on Windows to get the SUM written for you.
typing_hint: Type =SUM( then select the range with the mouse and close the bracket; do not forget the equals sign.
memory_story: A shopkeeper tips all the coins from a tray into one pile, and the pile's weight is the SUM.
visual: A column of numbers with a bold total line under it, drawn as a green rectangle with the formula.
emotion: Confidence from seeing the correct total appear without a calculator.
meaning: SUM adds all the numbers in the cells you give it.
native_meaning: SUM = जोड़ना, सारी संख्याओं का कुल
code: A1: 10¶A2: 20¶A3: 30¶A4: =SUM(A1:A3)
output: 60
explanation: SUM adds every number in the range. It ignores text and empty cells in the range. AutoSum, on the Home tab or with Alt+=, writes the SUM formula and guesses the range for you.
simple_explain: SUM sab number jod deta hai. Text ya khali cell ho to use chhod deta hai. Alt+= dabane se Excel khud formula likh deta hai.
analogy: Like emptying all small piles of money on a table into one big pile and counting that pile.
syntax: =SUM(number1, [number2], ...)
anatomy: = starts a formula | SUM = function name | ( ) = brackets | A1:A3 = numbers to add
example_1: A1: 10¶A2: 20¶A3: 30¶A4: =SUM(A1:A3) (shows 60, a simple range)
example_2: A1: 10¶A2: 20¶A3: 30¶B1: =SUM(A1:A3,5) (shows 65, a range plus a typed number)
example_3: A1: 10¶A2: n/a¶A3: 30¶B1: =SUM(A1:A3) (shows 40, the text n/a is ignored)
variation: =SUM(A:A) adds the whole column A¶Select A4 under the numbers and press Alt+= then Enter (Windows) for AutoSum¶Home tab > Editing group > AutoSum button (the sigma sign) does the same
used_with: AVERAGE, SUMIF, AutoSum, Alt+=
related: SUMIF, SUMIFS, AVERAGE, COUNT

code: A1: Pune¶A2: Agra¶A3: Pune¶A4: Delhi¶A5: Agra¶B1: =SORT(UNIQUE(A1:A5))
