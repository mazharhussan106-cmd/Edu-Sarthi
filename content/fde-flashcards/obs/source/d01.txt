@OBS-001
topic: 01 Ideas
level: Beginner
term: Logs, metrics and traces
cue: Which three kinds of telemetry describe what a running system is doing?
category: Core concept
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: Running systems cannot be seen directly, so engineers collect three kinds of signals that each answer different questions.
pro_tip: Start with metrics to notice a problem, use traces to find where, and use logs to learn why.
memory_story: Metrics are the speedometer, traces are the route map, and logs are the driver's diary.
visual: Three stacked panels showing a graph, a timeline of spans and lines of text.
emotion: The clarity of knowing which signal answers which question.
definition: Metrics are numbers measured over time, logs are timestamped records of events, and traces follow one request across the services it touches.
explanation: Metrics are cheap and good for trends and alerts, such as requests per second. Logs hold detail about single events, such as an error message with its context. Traces show the path and timing of one request through many services. Together they let you move from a symptom to its cause.
simple_explain: Metrics tell you something is wrong, traces show where, and logs explain why.
analogy: Metrics are the speedometer, traces are the route map, and logs are the driver's diary.
formula_rule: Metrics = what and how much. Traces = where. Logs = why.
example_1: A graph of error rate rising at ten past two.
example_2: A trace showing the slow call was the payment service.
example_3: A log line with the exact error and order id.
related: monitoring vs observability, structured logs, spans and traces, dashboards
word_family: telemetry, metric, log, trace, signal
where_used: Operations | Incident response | Platform design
mini_conversation: A: Users report slow checkouts. | B: Check the latency metric, then the trace, then the logs of the slow service.
misconception: ✗ Logs alone are enough to understand a system → ✓ Metrics and traces answer questions logs answer poorly
contrast: Metrics vs logs: metrics are cheap numbers over time, logs are detailed single events.
avoid: Do not rely on one signal type only.
memory_trick: Numbers notice, traces locate, logs explain.
real_project: Adding traces showed that a slow customer page waited on one third-party call.
real_interview: An interviewer may ask: What is the difference between logs, metrics and traces?
real_daily: It is like a speedometer, a route map and a diary.
mcq_question: Which signal follows one request across services?
mcq_options: A) A metric B) A dashboard C) A ticket D) A trace
mcq_answer: D
mcq_why: Traces connect the steps of a single request.
fill_blank: Numbers measured over time are called ____ → metrics
apply: You need to find which service made a request slow. Which signal? → A trace
explain_task: Explain the three signals in three lines.
own_task: Give one question that each signal answers best.

@OBS-002
topic: 01 Ideas
level: Intermediate
term: Monitoring vs observability
cue: Which two ideas differ in whether you check known problems or can investigate unknown ones?
category: Core concept
frequency: High
importance: 4
origin: Simple checks catch known failures, but complex systems fail in new ways, so observability aims to let you ask new questions without new code.
pro_tip: Build monitoring for known failure modes and observability for the surprises.
memory_story: Monitoring is a smoke alarm for known fires, and observability is a toolkit to investigate any smell.
visual: A fixed list of alarms beside a flexible search and drill-down tool.
emotion: The confidence of being able to investigate anything.
definition: Monitoring watches predefined signals and alerts on known conditions, while observability is the ability to understand internal behaviour from rich telemetry, including problems nobody predicted.
explanation: Monitoring answers known questions such as whether CPU is high. Observability needs detailed, high-context data that you can slice by many fields, such as user, version and region. It depends on good instrumentation and structured events. The two work together, since alerts tell you when to investigate.
simple_explain: Monitoring checks the known, and observability lets you explore the unknown.
analogy: Monitoring is a smoke alarm for known fires, and observability is a toolkit to investigate any smell.
formula_rule: Monitoring = known questions. Observability = new questions.
example_1: An alert when disk usage passes ninety percent.
example_2: Filtering traces by app version to find a bad release.
example_3: Grouping errors by customer to spot one affected account.
related: logs metrics and traces, alerts, structured logs, cardinality
word_family: monitoring, observability, instrumentation, high cardinality, drill-down
where_used: Platform engineering | Incidents | Tool choice
mini_conversation: A: The alerts are quiet but customers complain. | B: We need better observability to see what the alerts miss.
misconception: ✗ Observability is just a fancier word for monitoring → ✓ It is the ability to investigate new problems from rich data
contrast: Monitoring vs observability: known checks versus open investigation.
avoid: Do not buy tools instead of adding good instrumentation.
memory_trick: Alarm for the known, toolkit for the unknown.
real_project: Adding version and customer fields to events let the team find a bug affecting one customer.
real_interview: An interviewer may ask: How is observability different from monitoring?
real_daily: It is like an alarm plus a toolkit.
mcq_question: What does observability emphasise?
mcq_options: A) Only dashboards B) Only fixed alerts C) Investigating unknown problems from rich data D) Only logs
mcq_answer: C
mcq_why: It supports new questions without new code.
fill_blank: Adding telemetry code to an application is called ____ → instrumentation
apply: Alerts are quiet but one customer has errors. What helps? → Rich events you can filter by customer
explain_task: Explain monitoring versus observability in three lines.
own_task: List three fields to add to events to make them searchable.

@OBS-003
topic: 01 Ideas
level: Beginner
term: Health checks
cue: Which simple endpoints or probes tell you whether a service is alive and ready?
category: Core concept
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: Orchestrators and load balancers need a fast yes or no from each service, so services expose health endpoints.
pro_tip: Keep liveness cheap and local, and make readiness check what the service needs to serve traffic.
memory_story: A health check is a pulse and a readiness check is asking if the shop is open.
visual: A load balancer asking a service for its health and getting a green tick.
emotion: The comfort of automatic removal of sick instances.
definition: A health check is an endpoint or command that reports whether a service is alive and, separately, ready to handle requests.
explanation: Liveness answers whether the process should be restarted. Readiness answers whether traffic should be sent to it, including the few dependencies it cannot work without. Load balancers, Kubernetes and monitors call these regularly. Checks must be fast and must not overload dependencies. A deep check that fails on any dependency can take the whole fleet down at once.
simple_explain: Expose a cheap liveness check and a readiness check for dependencies, and let platforms use them.
analogy: A health check is a pulse, and readiness is asking whether the shop is open.
formula_rule: Liveness = restart me. Readiness = send me traffic.
example_1: A /healthz endpoint returning 200 quickly.
example_2: A /ready endpoint that checks the database connection.
example_3: A load balancer removing a failing instance.
related: Kubernetes probes, Docker HEALTHCHECK, load balancers, alerts
word_family: liveness, readiness, endpoint, probe, heartbeat
where_used: Load balancers | Orchestrators | Uptime monitors
mini_conversation: A: One database blip took every instance out of rotation. | B: The readiness check was too deep, so keep it narrower.
misconception: ✗ A health check should test every dependency deeply → ✓ Deep checks can cause a whole fleet to fail together
contrast: Liveness vs readiness: restart decision versus traffic decision.
avoid: Do not make liveness depend on outside systems.
memory_trick: Alive means restart, ready means route.
real_project: Narrowing a readiness check stopped repeated mass restarts during database slowdowns.
real_interview: An interviewer may ask: What is the difference between liveness and readiness?
real_daily: It is like a pulse and an open sign.
mcq_question: What does a readiness check decide?
mcq_options: A) Whether to send traffic B) Whether to delete the code C) Whether to rename the host D) Whether to buy storage
mcq_answer: A
mcq_why: Not-ready instances are skipped by routers.
fill_blank: A check telling a platform to restart a process is a ____ check → liveness
apply: An instance is slow to warm up. What stops traffic until it is ready? → A readiness check
explain_task: Explain health checks in three lines.
own_task: Design the liveness and readiness checks for an API with a database.

category: Core concept

pro_tip: Make one overview dashboard per service showing the golden signals first.

avoid: Do not rely on someone watching a dashboard to catch problems.

@OBS-005
topic: 01 Ideas
level: Beginner
term: Alerts
cue: How do you get told about problems that need action, instead of watching screens?
category: Core concept
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: Nobody can watch dashboards all day, so alerts notify people when signals cross meaningful thresholds.
pro_tip: Alert on symptoms users feel, and send each alert to a person who can act.
memory_story: An alert is a smoke alarm that rings in the right person's pocket.
visual: A graph crossing a threshold and a notification appearing on a phone.
emotion: The comfort of being warned before customers complain.
definition: An alert is an automatic notification triggered when a signal meets a rule, such as an error rate above a threshold for several minutes.
explanation: Good alerts are actionable, urgent and based on symptoms such as errors and latency. Rules use thresholds with durations to avoid noise. Alerts need an owner, a severity and a link to a runbook. Causes such as high CPU are better as warnings or dashboard items than pages. Test alerts to confirm they fire and reach the right people.
simple_explain: Alert on user-facing symptoms, route to an owner, and include what to do next.
analogy: An alert is a smoke alarm that rings in the right person's pocket.
formula_rule: Symptom + threshold + duration + owner + runbook.
example_1: Page when the error rate is above five percent for five minutes.
example_2: A ticket for disk usage above eighty percent.
example_3: A message with a link to the runbook.
related: alert fatigue, runbooks, SLOs, on-call
word_family: alert, threshold, severity, page, routing
where_used: On-call | Production systems | Customer operations
mini_conversation: A: We get fifty alerts a night. | B: Remove the ones that need no action and alert on symptoms.
misconception: ✗ Alert on every metric that moves → ✓ Alert only on conditions that need human action
contrast: Page vs ticket: a page needs action now, a ticket can wait.
avoid: Do not create alerts nobody can act on.
memory_trick: Each alert needs an owner and an action.
real_project: Cutting alerts to actionable symptoms gave on-call engineers quiet nights.
real_interview: An interviewer may ask: What makes a good alert?
real_daily: It is like a smoke alarm that reaches the right person.
mcq_question: Which alert is usually best to page on?
mcq_options: A) Slightly high CPU B) A full moon C) High error rate seen by users D) A new log line
mcq_answer: C
mcq_why: It reflects user pain and needs action.
fill_blank: A rule that waits several minutes before firing uses a ____ → duration
apply: CPU is at seventy percent but users are fine. Should you page? → No, make it a warning
explain_task: Explain good alerting in three lines.
own_task: Write three alert rules and decide page or ticket for each.
