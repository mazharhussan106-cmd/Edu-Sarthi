topic: 01 Ideas
level: Beginner
term: What RAG is
cue: Which pattern lets a model answer from your documents by retrieving relevant text first?
category: Core concept
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: Models do not know private or recent information, so retrieval fetches relevant passages and places them in the prompt before generation.
pro_tip: Think of RAG as an open-book exam for the model.
memory_story: RAG is a student who looks up the right pages before writing the answer.
visual: A question, a search step, retrieved passages and a final answer.
emotion: The comfort of answers backed by real documents.
definition: Retrieval-augmented generation retrieves relevant passages from a knowledge source for a user's question, adds them to the prompt, and has the model generate an answer grounded in them.
explanation: The pipeline has two phases. Offline, documents are split, embedded and indexed. Online, the question is embedded, similar chunks are retrieved, a prompt is built with the chunks, and the model answers, often with citations. RAG keeps knowledge outside the model so it can be updated and access-controlled.
simple_explain: Search your documents first, then let the model answer using what was found.
analogy: RAG is a student who looks up the right pages before writing the answer.
formula_rule: Retrieve, augment the prompt, generate.
example_1: A support bot answering from the help centre.
example_2: An assistant answering questions about internal policies.
example_3: A contract search tool with cited clauses.
related: embeddings, grounding, chunking, vector search
word_family: retrieval, generation, chunk, index, grounding
where_used: Knowledge assistants | Support | Enterprise search
mini_conversation: A: The model does not know our new policy. | B: Use RAG so it reads the policy before answering.
misconception: ✗ RAG trains the model on your documents → ✓ It only supplies relevant text at question time
contrast: RAG vs fine-tuning: RAG supplies knowledge at run time, fine-tuning changes the model.
avoid: Do not assume retrieval always finds the right passage.
memory_trick: Look up first, then write.
real_project: A RAG assistant answered from a customer's help centre with cited sources.
