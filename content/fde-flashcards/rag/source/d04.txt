cue: Which metrics show whether retrieval finds the right chunks?
category: Core concept
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: Retrieval limits answer quality, so it is tested on its own with labelled questions before judging the whole system.

emotion: The confidence of improvements that are measured.
definition: Retrieval evaluation compares retrieved results with labelled relevant chunks, using metrics such as recall at k, precision at k, mean reciprocal rank and nDCG.
explanation: Recall at k is the share of relevant chunks found in the top k. A simpler hit rate counts the questions where at least one relevant chunk appears in the top k. Precision is the share of retrieved chunks that are relevant. Mean reciprocal rank rewards placing the first relevant result high. Labels can be made by experts or generated carefully and reviewed. Run the evaluation after each change of chunking, embeddings, filters or rerankers.
simple_explain: Label questions with correct chunks, then measure recall and ranking after each change.
analogy: Evaluating retrieval is a quiz that checks whether the librarian fetches the right books.
formula_rule: Hit rate at k = questions with a hit in the top k ÷ all questions.
example_1: Recall at 5 rising from 70 to 85 percent.
example_2: MRR showing the right chunk is usually first.
example_3: A failing question list.
related: when RAG fails, top-k, reranking, AI evaluation

misconception: ✗ Answer quality alone shows retrieval quality → ✓ Retrieval should be measured separately
contrast: Recall vs precision: found relevant items versus how clean the results are.
avoid: Do not tune without a labelled set.

term: Agentic RAG
cue: How can a model decide when and how to search, instead of always retrieving once?
category: Core concept
frequency: Medium
importance: 4
origin: Complex questions need several searches and checks, so the model is given retrieval as a tool and can plan its own steps.
pro_tip: Use agentic RAG only when simple retrieval cannot handle the questions.
memory_story: Agentic RAG is a researcher who searches, reads, refines the question and searches again.
visual: A loop of plan, search, read and decide.
emotion: The excitement of deeper answers and the caution of extra cost.

Set step limits, log every search, validate tool arguments and keep permission filters in the tool, not the model. Compare against a simple pipeline, since many questions do not need it.
simple_explain: Give the model a search tool and limits, and use it when one retrieval is not enough.
analogy: Agentic RAG is a researcher who searches, reads, refines and searches again.
formula_rule: Plan, search, assess, repeat within limits.

avoid: Do not put permission checks in the model's hands.

category: Core concept

emotion: The curiosity of questions that follow links.
definition: Graph-based retrieval builds a graph of entities and relationships extracted from documents, then retrieves by traversing connections and summaries, so questions about relations and themes can be answered with linked evidence.
explanation: Extraction of entities and relations is done with models and is imperfect and costly. Queries can follow paths, such as which suppliers affect this product. Community summaries support broad questions across a whole corpus. Graphs need updates when documents change. They suit connected domains such as compliance, supply chains and research, but many problems do not need them.
simple_explain: Extract entities and relations, then retrieve by following links.
analogy: A knowledge graph is a map of people, places and links between them.
formula_rule: Entities + relations + traversal + summaries.
example_1: Finding all contracts linked to one supplier.
example_2: A theme summary across a large corpus.
example_3: A path from a product to its components.
