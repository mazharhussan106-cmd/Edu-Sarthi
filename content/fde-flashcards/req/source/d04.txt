level: Intermediate
term: Handover document
cue: Which document lets another team take over your work without you?
category: Document
frequency: High
importance: 5
origin: People change roles and projects end, so everything the next team needs must be written down and checked.
pro_tip: Do the handover as a short walkthrough, and then ask the receiver to run something themselves.
memory_story: A handover document is the baton that must be handed over, not dropped.
visual: A folder with architecture, access, runbook, contacts and open issues.
emotion: The pride of leaving work in good hands.
definition: A handover document gives a receiving team what they need to own a system or project, including what was built, how it works, how to run and fix it, who to contact, and what remains open.
explanation: Include a summary of goals and the final scope, an architecture sketch, where code and configuration live, how access is granted without sharing secrets, links to the runbook and decision log, known issues, planned next steps and contacts. Walk through it with the receiving team and let them try the main tasks. Agree a short period of support after the handover.
simple_explain: Write everything a new team needs to run and fix what you built.
analogy: A handover document is the baton that must be handed over, not dropped.
formula_rule: What, how, where, who, what is open.
example_1: A list of systems with owners and contacts.
example_2: Links to the runbook and decision log.
example_3: A list of known issues and next steps.
related: README, runbook, decision log, meeting notes
word_family: handover, ownership, access, contacts, support period
where_used: Project end | Role changes | Customer go-live
mini_conversation: A: Who owns the integration after you leave? | B: The customer's team, using the handover document and a walkthrough.
misconception: ✗ A handover is a one-hour meeting → ✓ It needs documents, a walkthrough and the receiver trying tasks
contrast: Handover vs README: full ownership transfer versus a start guide.
avoid: Do not hand over passwords in a document.
memory_trick: Document it, walk through it, let them try.

contrast: Status vs report: a short update versus a full analysis.
avoid: Do not hide a blocker until it becomes a crisis.
memory_trick: Done, next, blocked, and what you need.

category: Skill
frequency: High

misconception: ✗ Saying yes always keeps the customer happy → ✓ A false yes damages trust when it fails
contrast: A flat no vs a helpful no: closing the door versus offering another route.
avoid: Do not say maybe when you mean no.
memory_trick: No to the ask, yes to the need.

category: Skill
frequency: High

formula_rule: Say it, write it, check it back, revisit it.

word_family: expectation, alignment, limits, dependency, confirmation
where_used: Kick-off | Demos | Go-live

misconception: ✗ Under-promising is enough → ✓ Clear, shared understanding matters more
contrast: Expectation vs promise: what someone believes versus what was agreed.
avoid: Do not leave limits unsaid hoping nobody asks.
memory_trick: Say it, write it, check it.
