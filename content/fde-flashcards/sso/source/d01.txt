@SSO-001
topic: 01 Basics
level: Beginner
term: Authentication vs authorisation
cue: Which two questions separate proving who you are from deciding what you may do?
category: Core concept
frequency: Very high
importance: 5
origin: Early systems mixed login and permissions, which caused confusion and flaws, so the two ideas were separated.
pro_tip: Always say which one you mean, since many bugs come from confusing them.
memory_story: Authentication is showing your passport at the border, and authorisation is the visa that says where you may go.
visual: A door with an ID check and then a list of allowed rooms.
emotion: The clarity of separating identity from permission.
definition: Authentication verifies who a user or system is, and authorisation decides which actions and resources that identity is allowed to use.
explanation: Authentication uses passwords, codes, keys or biometrics. Authorisation uses roles, scopes, policies and attributes. A user can be authenticated but still denied. OAuth is about authorisation, while OpenID Connect adds authentication. HTTP status 401 means not authenticated, and 403 means not allowed.
simple_explain: Authentication answers who you are, authorisation answers what you can do.
analogy: Authentication is showing your passport, and authorisation is the visa for where you may go.
formula_rule: AuthN = who. AuthZ = what.
example_1: Logging in with a password and a code.
example_2: A user who can read but not delete records.
example_3: A 403 error after a successful login.
related: SSO, MFA, RBAC vs ABAC, scopes and claims
word_family: authentication, authorisation, identity, permission, access
where_used: Every application | Security reviews | API design
mini_conversation: A: The user logged in but sees an error. | B: They are authenticated but not authorised for that page.
misconception: ✗ Logging in gives access to everything → ✓ Authorisation still decides what each identity may do
contrast: 401 vs 403: 401 means not authenticated, 403 means authenticated but not allowed.
avoid: Do not check only that a user is logged in before sensitive actions.
memory_trick: Who are you, then what may you do.
real_project: Separating the two checks fixed a page that let any logged-in user view admin data.
real_interview: An interviewer may ask: What is the difference between authentication and authorisation?
real_daily: It is like a passport and a visa.
mcq_question: Which status means the caller is known but not allowed?
mcq_options: A) 301 B) 401 C) 200 D) 403
mcq_answer: D
mcq_why: 403 is forbidden after identification.
fill_blank: Verifying who someone is is called ____ → authentication
apply: A logged-in user opens an admin page and is blocked. Which check failed? → Authorisation
explain_task: Explain the two ideas in three lines.
own_task: Give one example of each in an online shop.
