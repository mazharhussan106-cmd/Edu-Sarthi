sentence: Hi Ananya, I'm Rohan from [Company]. I can see you're asking about order 48213, and I'll look into it right away.
speaker: Agent
situation: A customer named Ananya has started a chat about a late order and typed her order number before any agent joined.
recall_cue: The customer has already typed her name and order number. How do you greet her so she knows a real person has read her message?
stress: HI a-NAN-ya · i'm RO-han from COM-pa-ny · i can SEE you're ASK-ing a-BOUT OR-der four-eight-two-one-THREE · and i'll LOOK in-to it RIGHT a-WAY
linking: If read aloud: look_into_it → "loo-kin-tu-wit" · right_away → "ri-ta-way" · I_can_see → "ai-kn-SEE"
intonation: If read aloud: a friendly rise on "Ananya", a short pause after your name, then a steady falling tone on "right away" so it sounds confident.
indian_pron_tip: If read aloud: finish "asking" with a soft "ng" and no extra hard "g", and say "order" with a light English "d" (tongue behind the top teeth), not a heavy retroflex one.
register: Friendly
tone: Warm, personal, confident
importance: 5
customer_emotion: Hopeful but slightly impatient after waiting for an agent to join.
memory_hook: Picture a shop assistant who smiles, says your name and is already holding your parcel slip: her name, my name, her issue, my action.
hindi_meaning: नमस्ते अनन्या, मैं [Company] से रोहन हूँ। मैं देख रहा/रही हूँ कि आप ऑर्डर 48213 के बारे में पूछ रही हैं, मैं अभी इसे देखता/देखती हूँ।
hindi_roman: Namaste Ananya, main [Company] se Rohan hoon. Main dekh raha/rahi hoon ki aap order 48213 ke baare mein pooch rahi hain, main abhi ise dekhta/dekhti hoon.
simple_meaning: Hello Ananya, my name is Rohan and I work for the company. I have read your question about your order, and I will start checking it now.
why_it_works: Her first name and her own order number prove a real person read the message, so she does not need to explain again. "I'll look into it right away" gives her the next step in the very first line.
key_chunk: I can see you're asking about ___, and I'll ___ right away.
grammar_pattern: I can see + (that) + you're + verb-ing · I'll + base verb + right away
grammar_note: "I can see you're asking" shows you have read her message; "I'll" is a promise you make right now.
tone_note: The first name and "I'm Rohan" make it personal and human. "Dear Customer, how may I assist you?" ignores what she already wrote and reads like a machine.
watch_out: ✗ I can see you asking about order 48213, and I'll look in it right away. → ✓ I can see you're asking about order 48213, and I'll look into it right away.
dont_say: "Dear Customer, please wait." It is cold, ignores her name and her message, and gives no next step.
positive_swap: Instead of "Please wait while I go through your query", say "I can see you're asking about order 48213, and I'll look into it right away."
alt_1: Hi Ananya, thanks for waiting. I'm Rohan, and I'm checking order 48213 for you now.
alt_2: Hello Ananya, Rohan here from [Company]. I've read your message about order 48213 and I'm on it.
more_formal: Good afternoon, Ms Rao. My name is Rohan, and I'll be looking into order 48213 for you today.
ask_next: I can see it left our warehouse on Monday. Is it still going to the address saved on your account?
replies_you_may_hear: Hi, thanks. · Finally! It was supposed to come on Friday. · Yes, can you tell me where it is?
if_customer_pushes: Customer: "Why did I wait so long for someone to reply?" → Agent: "I'm sorry about the wait, Ananya. I'm here now, and I've already opened your order."
cultural_note: Many Indian agents open with "Ma'am" or "Sir" and a fixed question. International chat customers usually expect their first name and a short, personal opening that mentions their issue.
channel_note: Voice: "Thank you for calling [Company], this is Rohan. How can I help?" Email: "Hi Ananya," on its own line, then your answer.
when_not_to_use: Do not switch to her first name if she signed with a title and surname, such as "Dr Mehta"; follow the name she uses.
mini_conversation: Customer: Hi, my order 48213 hasn't arrived. | Agent: Hi Ananya, I'm Rohan from [Company]. I can see you're asking about order 48213, and I'll look into it right away. | Customer: Thanks, it was due on Friday. | Agent: I can see it left our warehouse on Monday. I'm checking the courier update now.
source_rule: Chat: greet with your name, use the customer's first name naturally, and reply fast.

mcq_why: B uses her name, gives yours and shows you read her message by naming the order and the next step. D is tempting because it is friendly and personal, but it asks what she needs when she has already told you. A and C ignore what she typed.

intonation: If read aloud: pause briefly after each number, fall on every step, and lift slightly on "let me know" to invite a reply.

simple_meaning: Do these three things in order, one at a time, and send me a message when you get to the third one.
why_it_works: Numbered steps with one action each are easy to follow on a small screen and cut down follow-up questions. Asking the customer to report back keeps you in step with them.
key_chunk: Here's how to ___: 1. ___ 2. ___ 3. ___ Let me know when you reach step ___.
grammar_pattern: Here's how + to + base verb · Imperative (Tap + noun) · Let me know when + you + present simple
grammar_note: Each step starts with a command verb like "Tap"; in instructions this is polite and clear, not rude.
tone_note: Short commands are normal in instructions. A long paragraph with "you need to first go to the login screen and then…" feels heavy and is easy to misread.
watch_out: ✗ You have to go login then forgot password then type email. → ✓ 1. Tap Log in. 2. Tap Forgot password. 3. Type your email address.
dont_say: "Just follow the steps on the website." It sends the customer away with no help and feels dismissive.
positive_swap: Instead of "You have to go to the login page, click forgot password and type your email", say "Here's how to reset your password: 1. Tap Log in. 2. Tap Forgot password. 3. Type your email address."
alt_1: Let's reset it together. 1. Open the login screen. 2. Choose Forgot password. 3. Type your email address. Tell me what you see.
alt_2: Three quick steps: 1. Login screen. 2. Forgot password. 3. Your email address. Let me know once you're there.
more_formal: To reset your password, please follow these steps: 1. Open the login screen. 2. Select Forgot password. 3. Enter your email address.
ask_next: Can you see the "Send reset link" button under the email box?
replies_you_may_hear: Done, I'm on step 3. · I can't see Forgot password. · Should I do this on the app or the website?
if_customer_pushes: Customer: "I don't have time for all this." → Agent: "I know you're in a hurry. It takes about a minute, and I'll stay with you at each step."
cultural_note: Indian agents often write the whole process as one long sentence joined by "then". International customers expect numbered, one-action steps they can scan quickly.
channel_note: Voice: give one step, wait for "done", then the next. Email: number the steps on separate lines and add a screenshot if possible.
when_not_to_use: Do not send a numbered list before you know which device or app version the customer uses; ask first.
mini_conversation: Customer: I can't log in to the app. | Agent: Here's how to reset your password: 1. Tap Log in. 2. Tap Forgot password. 3. Type your email address. Let me know when you reach step 3. | Customer: Okay, I'm there. | Agent: Great. Now tap "Send reset link". The email usually arrives within a minute.
source_rule: Number multi-step instructions, one action per step, to cut follow-up questions.

fill_answer: how

Tap Log in. 2. Tap Forgot password. 3. Type your email address. | D) 1. Open the app, go to the login screen, tap Forgot password and type your email.

mcq_why: C gives one action per numbered step, so the customer can follow it and report back. D is numbered, but it packs four actions into one step, A is one long line, and B sends the customer away without help.
