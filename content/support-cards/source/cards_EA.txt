sentence: Thank you for setting out your complaint so clearly, Ms Rao. I'm sorry your replacement laptop arrived with a cracked screen, and I have booked a courier to collect it on Thursday.
speaker: Agent
situation: A customer has sent a long, formal complaint by email after her replacement laptop arrived damaged, and you are writing the first reply.
recall_cue: A customer has written a careful, formal complaint about a damaged replacement. What do you thank her for, what exactly do you apologise for, and what action do you name?
stress: THANK-you · for-SET-ting-OUT · your-com-PLAINT · so-CLEAR-ly · Miz-RAO · i'm-SOR-ry · your-re-PLACE-ment-LAP-top · a-RIVED · with-a-CRACKED-SCREEN · and-i-have-BOOKED · a-COUR-i-er · to-col-LECT-it · on-THURS-day
linking: If read aloud: setting_out → "set-ting-OUT" · booked_a → "book-ta" · collect_it → "co-LEC-tit"
intonation: If read aloud: fall on "clearly", pause, then a steady falling tone on "cracked screen", and end firm and low on "Thursday" so the action sounds decided.
indian_pron_tip: If read aloud, start "Thursday" with "th" (tongue lightly between the teeth), not "Tursday"; and "booked" ends in a short "t" sound ("bookt"), not "book-ed".
register: Formal
tone: Respectful, specific, accountable
importance: 5
customer_emotion: Let down twice and ready to escalate if the reply is vague.
memory_hook: Picture three stamps on her letter: THANK for the detail, SORRY for the exact damage, BOOKED for the action already done.
hindi_meaning: अपनी शिकायत इतने स्पष्ट ढंग से बताने के लिए धन्यवाद, सुश्री राव। मुझे खेद है कि आपका रिप्लेसमेंट लैपटॉप टूटी हुई स्क्रीन के साथ पहुँचा, और मैंने गुरुवार को उसे ले जाने के लिए कूरियर बुक कर दिया है।
hindi_roman: Apni shikayat itne spasht dhang se batane ke liye dhanyavaad, Sushri Rao. Mujhe khed hai ki aapka replacement laptop tooti hui screen ke saath pahuncha, aur maine guruvaar ko use le jaane ke liye courier book kar diya hai.
simple_meaning: Thank you for explaining the problem well. I'm sorry the new laptop came broken. A courier will pick it up on Thursday.
why_it_works: Thanking the customer for the detail treats the complaint as useful, not as an attack. The apology names the exact fault and is followed at once by an action already taken, which is what a formal complainant is looking for.
key_chunk: I'm sorry your ___, and I have ___.
grammar_pattern: Thank you for + -ing · I'm sorry (that) + past simple · I have + past participle (action already done)
grammar_note: "I have booked" (present perfect) shows the action is already done, which is stronger than "I will try to book".
tone_note: Using her title and surname matches her formal email. "Sorry for the inconvenience" would sound scripted after a second failure and could make her angrier.
watch_out: ✗ We are sorry for the inconvenience caused to you. → ✓ I'm sorry your replacement laptop arrived with a cracked screen.
dont_say: "We regret the inconvenience caused." — it hides who is sorry and for what, and it reads like a template.
positive_swap: Instead of "Your complaint has been noted", write "Thank you for setting out your complaint so clearly; I have already booked a courier."
alt_1: Thank you for your detailed email, Ms Rao. I'm sorry the replacement laptop reached you damaged; a courier will collect it on Thursday.
alt_2: I'm sorry that your second laptop also arrived with a fault, Ms Rao. I have arranged a Thursday collection, and I will email you on Friday to confirm what happens next.
more_formal: Thank you for setting out your concerns in such detail, Ms Rao. Please accept my apology that your replacement laptop arrived damaged; I have arranged for a courier to collect it on Thursday.
ask_next: Could you tell me whether a morning or an afternoon collection on Thursday suits you better?
replies_you_may_hear: Thursday is fine, but I want a refund, not another laptop. · Why should I trust the next one? · Thank you, that is the first useful reply I have had.
if_customer_pushes: Customer: "I don't want another replacement. I want my money back." → Agent: "I understand, after two faulty laptops. I will check your refund options today and email you by 5 pm, so you know exactly what happens once the courier collects the laptop on Thursday."
cultural_note: Many Indian office replies open with "This is with reference to your mail dated…". International customers may not expect this; they look for thanks, a specific apology and the action in the first two sentences.
channel_note: Voice: say it more simply and agree a collection time. Chat: split it into two short messages. Email: keep the title and surname if the customer used them.
when_not_to_use: Do not thank someone for setting out the complaint "so clearly" if the email was short and furious; it can sound sarcastic.
mini_conversation: Customer: I am writing to complain that the replacement laptop you sent is also faulty. The screen was cracked when I opened the box. | Agent: Thank you for setting out your complaint so clearly, Ms Rao. I'm sorry your replacement laptop arrived with a cracked screen, and I have booked a courier to collect it on Thursday. | Customer: What happens after that? | Agent: I will ask our warehouse to inspect your next laptop before it ships, and I will email you the tracking number by Friday.
source_rule: Apologise specifically, take responsibility, then offer a fix (Lewicki et al., 2016: responsibility and repair rank highest).
fill_blank: I'm sorry your replacement laptop arrived with a cracked screen, and I have ____ (book) a courier to collect it on Thursday.
fill_answer: booked
mcq_question: A customer sends a formal complaint about a damaged replacement. Which opening line is best?
mcq_options: A) We regret any inconvenience caused to you and assure you that your complaint is being looked into. | B) Your complaint has been noted and passed to the relevant team, who will contact you in due course. | C) I'm sorry your replacement arrived with a cracked screen, and I have booked a courier for Thursday. | D) Thank you for your email. We take every complaint seriously and apologise for any inconvenience caused.
mcq_answer: C
mcq_why: C names the exact fault and an action already taken. B is tempting because it sounds formal, but it names no problem and gives no time. A and D are stock apologies that name no fault.
reorder_task: courier / a / have / I / booked / Thursday / on / collect / to / it
reorder_answer: I have booked a courier to collect it on Thursday.

hindi_to_english_answer: I'm sorry your order arrived in the wrong size, and I have booked an exchange for you.
whats_next_customer: Why did this happen twice?
whats_next_reply: That is a fair question. I have asked our warehouse to check how both units were packed, and I will tell you what they find by Friday.

why_it_works: It gives a real reason (a date) instead of "policy", softens the refusal with empathy, and ends on two things the customer can actually choose, so the email does not end on "no".
key_chunk: As ___, we can't ___. I understand ___, but I can offer you ___.

grammar_note: "As" here means "because"; it introduces the reason before the decision.
tone_note: Putting the reason first and the options last leaves the customer with something to say yes to. "This is not possible as per our policy" sounds like a wall.
watch_out: ✗ As per policy, return is not possible. → ✓ As the 30-day return period ended on 2 May, we can't accept a return now.
dont_say: "Unfortunately, we cannot help you with this request." — it closes the conversation and offers nothing.
positive_swap: Instead of "We cannot accept your return", write "What I can offer you is full store credit, or a free repair if the machine has a fault."
alt_1: The return period closed on 2 May, so a refund is no longer available. I know you were hoping to return the machine, but I can arrange full store credit, or a free repair if it has a fault.
alt_2: Because the machine was delivered more than 30 days ago, we can't take it back. I'm sorry this is not the news you wanted, but we can give you store credit, or repair it free of charge if it has a fault.
more_formal: As the 30-day return period ended on 2 May, we are unable to accept a return. I appreciate that this is not the outcome you had hoped for; however, I would be glad to offer full store credit or, if the machine is faulty, a free repair.
ask_next: Please let me know which option you prefer, and I will set it up within one working day.

cultural_note: "As per policy, it is not possible" is common in Indian service writing. International customers may read it as hiding behind rules; they expect the actual reason and an alternative.
channel_note: Voice: say the empathy line slowly and pause before the options. Chat: send the reason and the options as two short messages. Email: put the options on two separate lines.
when_not_to_use: Do not use this before you know why the customer wants to return the item; if the machine is faulty, check the warranty first, because a fault is usually a repair or replacement case.
mini_conversation: Customer: I'd like to return the coffee machine I bought in March. | Agent: As the 30-day return period ended on 2 May, we can't accept a return now. I understand this isn't the answer you were hoping for, but I can offer you full store credit, or a free repair if the machine has a fault. | Customer: Store credit is fine. | Agent: Thank you. I will add the credit to your account today and email you a confirmation by 5 pm.
source_rule: Saying no by email: reason, then empathy, then an alternative, then a follow-up; never a bare "No". Don't hide behind "policy" (derived advice).

simple_meaning: We took your money three times by mistake. It was our fault. I have sent back the two extra payments today, and I will send you proof by 5 pm.
why_it_works: It says clearly whose fault it was, which matters most in an apology, and pairs it with a repair already made and a time for proof. The customer does not have to chase anything.
key_chunk: I'm sorry we ___; that was our mistake. I have ___, and I will ___ by ___.

grammar_note: "That was our mistake" is a short, complete sentence that takes responsibility without excuses.
tone_note: The short, plain sentences sound sincere. Long explanations about "technical issues" before the apology sound like excuses and make the customer angrier.

positive_swap: Instead of "The error occurred due to a system issue", write "That was our mistake, and I have refunded the extra payments today."
alt_1: I'm very sorry: we charged you three times for one order, and that should never have happened. I have refunded both extra payments of $129 today.
alt_2: Charging you three times for one order was our error, and I apologise. I have refunded the two extra payments, and I will send you the bank reference by 5 pm today.

channel_note: Voice: say the apology first, slowly, before any detail. Chat: send the apology and the refund as two short messages. Email: put the refund amount and the time for proof on separate lines.
when_not_to_use: Do not say "that was our mistake" before you have checked the facts; if the bank caused the extra charges, apologise for the worry and explain what you found.

why_it_works: "Should this not…" is a formal way of saying "If this is not…". The customer is giving you one last chance with a clear deadline. They want a real fix, or at least a clear plan, before Friday.
key_chunk: Should this not be ___ by ___, I will ___.
grammar_pattern: Should + subject + not + be + past participle (inverted conditional) + by + day, + I will + verb
grammar_note: Starting with "Should" instead of "If" is a formal way to make a condition.
tone_note: The inverted "should" sounds cool and decided. Replying "We are trying our best" ignores the deadline; the customer needs a clear plan before Friday.
watch_out: ✗ (learner reads "Should" as advice) The customer says we should fix it. → ✓ "Should this not be resolved" means "If this is not resolved".
dont_say: "We will try our best to resolve it soon." — it ignores the customer's Friday deadline and promises nothing.
positive_swap: Instead of "We will try to fix it soon", write "I will give you a full update by Thursday at noon, before your deadline."
alt_1: Thank you for being clear about Friday. Our engineers are working on your account now, and I will update you by Thursday at noon.
alt_2: A week without your service is too long, and I'm sorry. Your case is now marked urgent, and I will email you by Thursday at noon, with your refund options if the fix is not ready.
more_formal: I appreciate that you need this resolved by Friday. I have escalated your case as urgent and will update you by Thursday at noon; should the issue remain, I will set out your refund options in full.
ask_next: I understand you need this fixed by Friday. I have raised your case as urgent, and I will update you by Thursday at noon with either the fix or your refund options.
replies_you_may_hear: Thursday is fine. · I've heard that before. · Just cancel it now.
if_customer_pushes: Customer: "Just cancel it now." → Agent: "Of course, and I'm sorry it has come to this. I can cancel it today. Before I do, would you like me to check what refund you are due for the days the service did not work?"
cultural_note: Learners who know "should" only as advice may misread "Should this not…". In formal British and international emails it simply means "if this is not…", so treat it as a deadline, not a suggestion.
channel_note: Voice: the spoken version is "If this isn't fixed by Friday, I'm cancelling." Chat: confirm the deadline and give a time for your next update. Email: reply before the deadline with a dated plan.
when_not_to_use: Do not promise a fix by Friday unless the technical team has confirmed it; promise an update instead.
mini_conversation: Customer: Should this not be resolved by Friday, I will cancel my subscription and request a full refund. | Agent: I understand you need this fixed by Friday. I have raised your case as urgent and will update you by Thursday at noon. | Customer: And if it is still broken then? | Agent: Then I will cancel the subscription for you and process your refund the same day, so you will not need to ask again.
source_rule: Set expectations: use "I will", plus a time, instead of "we'll try" or "hopefully soon".
fill_blank: ____ this not be resolved by Friday, I will cancel my subscription and request a full refund.

mcq_question: A customer writes, "Should this not be resolved by Friday, I will cancel." What does "Should this not be resolved" mean?
mcq_options: A) You ought to resolve this before Friday arrives. | B) If this has still not been resolved by Friday. | C) This should never have been left unresolved. | D) I am not sure this can be resolved by Friday.
mcq_answer: B
mcq_why: B: in formal English, starting with "Should" makes a condition, like "If". A is tempting because "should" usually means advice, but here the word order shows it is a condition. C and D change the meaning.
