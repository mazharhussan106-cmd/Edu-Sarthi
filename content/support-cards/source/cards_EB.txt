speaker: Agent
situation: You are sending the first update on a customer's refund, and your helpdesk lets you write the subject line of the email.
recall_cue: Your email is about a refund and has a ticket number. What short subject tells the customer exactly what is inside?
stress: UP-date · on-your-REE-fund-re-QUEST · TICK-et-NUM-ber · five-five-one-oh-TWO
linking: If read aloud: update_on → "up-day-ton" · refund_request → "ree-fun-dri-kwest"
intonation: If read aloud: a subject line is a label, so read it flat and falling, with a small pause before the ticket number.
indian_pron_tip: If read aloud, "update" is UP-date with stress on the first part, and the final "t" in "request" and "ticket" should be clear, not dropped.
register: Neutral
tone: Clear, specific, businesslike
importance: 4
customer_emotion: Waiting for money back and checking the inbox for news.
memory_hook: A subject line is the label on a box: the customer should know what is inside without opening the lid.
hindi_meaning: आपकी रिफ़ंड रिक्वेस्ट पर अपडेट – टिकट #55102
hindi_roman: Aapki refund request par update – ticket #55102
simple_meaning: This email tells you the latest news about the money you asked to get back.
why_it_works: The customer sees the topic and the ticket number before opening the email, so they can find it again and know it is not spam or a new problem.
key_chunk: Update on your ___ – Ticket #___
grammar_pattern: Noun (Update) + on + your + noun phrase + – + Ticket + number
grammar_note: Subject lines are short labels, so they skip "This is" and usually have no full stop.
tone_note: A specific, calm label sounds professional. A subject like "URGENT!!!" or "Important information" sounds like spam and can worry the customer.
watch_out: ✗ Regarding your query → ✓ Update on your refund request – Ticket #55102
dont_say: "Important information" — it says nothing about the content and looks like spam or a scam email.
positive_swap: Instead of "Your complaint", write "Update on your refund request – Ticket #55102".
alt_1: Your refund for order 62408 – Ticket #55102
alt_2: Refund update for order 62408 (Ticket #55102)
more_formal: Update regarding your refund request – Ticket #55102
ask_next: Hi Daniel, I have an update on your refund for order 62408.
replies_you_may_hear: Thanks, when will I get the money? · Is this about my old order? · Got it, thank you.
if_customer_pushes: Customer: "Why do you keep changing the subject line? I can't find your emails." → Agent: "I'm sorry the emails were hard to find. I'll keep the same subject line from now on so all our emails stay together."
cultural_note: Many Indian office emails use "Regarding your query" or "Reg: complaint". International customers expect a specific topic plus the ticket number.
channel_note: Voice and chat: no subject line; say the topic in the first sentence instead. Email: if you reply in the customer's thread, keep their subject and the "Re:".
when_not_to_use: Do not change the subject when replying inside an existing thread; keep the customer's subject so the emails stay together.
mini_conversation: Customer: I asked for a refund last week. | Agent: Subject: Update on your refund request – Ticket #55102. Hi Daniel, your refund is approved. | Customer: Great, when will I see it? | Agent: It should reach your card within five working days.
source_rule: Email subject: specific, short and with the ticket number; keep it when replying.
fill_blank: Update on your refund request – ____ (starts with T) #55102
fill_answer: Ticket
mcq_question: You are writing the first update on a customer's refund. Which subject line is best?
mcq_options: A) Important information on your account – Ticket #55102 | B) Update on your refund request – Ticket #55102 | C) Reg: your refund query – see details below | D) URGENT – PLEASE READ – Refund Ticket #55102
mcq_answer: B
mcq_why: B names the topic and the ticket number in a calm label. A has a ticket number but does not say what the email is about, C has no ticket number and uses an office abbreviation, and D is in capital letters, which looks like spam.
reorder_task: refund / Update / Ticket / your / on / request / 55102
reorder_answer: Update on your refund request – Ticket #55102

indian_pron_tip: If read aloud, "this" starts with a soft "th" (tongue lightly between the teeth), not "dis"; "support" has the stress on PORT.
register: Friendly
tone: Warm, personal, professional
importance: 4

why_it_works: Using the customer's name shows a person read the email, and giving your own name tells the customer who to reply to.
key_chunk: Hi ___, this is ___ from ___.
grammar_pattern: Hi + first name + , + this is + your name + from + team
grammar_note: In emails we write "this is Meera", not "I am Meera speaking".
tone_note: "Hi" plus a first name is friendly and normal in modern support. "Dear Customer" sounds like a machine, and "Respected Sir" sounds old-fashioned to many readers.
watch_out: ✗ Dear Customer, I am Meera speaking from support. → ✓ Hi Daniel, this is Meera from the [Company] support team.
dont_say: "Dear Customer," — it shows nobody looked at the customer's name and feels like a mass mailing.
positive_swap: Instead of "Dear Customer", write "Hi Daniel, this is Meera from the [Company] support team."

if_customer_pushes: Customer: "Is this an automatic reply?" → Agent: "No, I'm Meera and I've read your email myself. I'm looking into your order now."
cultural_note: Many Indian emails start with "Respected Sir" or "Dear Sir/Madam". International customers usually expect their name; use "Hi" plus first name if they signed that way, or "Dear Mr/Ms" plus surname if they wrote formally.
channel_note: Voice: "Thank you for calling [Company], this is Meera." Chat: "Hi Daniel, I'm Meera." Email: greeting and your name in the first line, then a blank line before the message.
when_not_to_use: Do not use "Hi" plus first name when the customer wrote formally and signed with a title and surname; use "Dear Mr Brooks" instead.

when_not_to_use: Do not send a holding reply when you can answer the whole question now; just answer it.
