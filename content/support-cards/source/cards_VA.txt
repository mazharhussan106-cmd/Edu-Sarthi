sentence: I can hear how frustrating this has been, and you have every right to be upset. Let me find out what went wrong and put it right.
speaker: Agent
situation: A customer's internet has been down for three days, and they open the call by shouting at you.
recall_cue: The caller is shouting about a problem that has gone on for days. How do you name their feeling and move to action without arguing back?
stress: i can HEAR how frus-TRAY-ting this has BEEN · you have EV-ery RIGHT to be up-SET · let me FIND out what went WRONG · and PUT it RIGHT
linking: has_been → "hez-bin" · and_you → "an-joo" · find_out → "fine-dout" · put_it → "pu-tit"
intonation: Low, slow and steady; do not match the caller's volume. Falling tone on "upset", a short pause, then a firmer falling tone on "put it right" to show action is starting.
indian_pron_tip: "Frustrating" is stressed on the second syllable, frus-TRAY-ting, not with equal beats. Make the "tr" with the tongue just behind the top teeth, not a heavy retroflex ṭ.
register: Neutral
tone: Calm, steady acknowledgement
importance: 5
customer_emotion: Angry, exhausted and expecting to be brushed off again.
memory_hook: Think of a fire: you do not throw words at it; first you name the heat, then you pick up the hose.
hindi_meaning: मैं समझ सकता/सकती हूँ कि यह कितना परेशान करने वाला रहा है, और आपका नाराज़ होना बिल्कुल जायज़ है। मुझे पता लगाने दीजिए कि क्या गड़बड़ हुई, और मैं उसे ठीक करूँगा/करूँगी।
hindi_roman: Main samajh sakta/sakti hoon ki yeh kitna pareshaan karne wala raha hai, aur aapka naraaz hona bilkul jaayaz hai. Mujhe pata lagane dijiye ki kya gadbad hui, aur main use theek karoonga/karoongi.
simple_meaning: I understand you are very annoyed, and you are right to feel that way. I will find the problem and fix it.
why_it_works: It names the feeling instead of arguing with it, agrees the anger is fair, and moves straight to action. The caller hears that you are on their side and that something will now happen.
key_chunk: I can hear how ___ this has been. Let me ___.
grammar_pattern: I can hear + how + adjective + this has been · Let me + base verb
grammar_note: "Has been" (present perfect) shows the problem started in the past and is still going on.
tone_note: Agreeing that the anger is fair lowers it. "There's no need to shout" or "Please calm down" tells the caller they are wrong to feel angry and usually makes them louder.
watch_out: ✗ Sir, please calm down, I am understanding your problem. → ✓ I can hear how frustrating this has been. Let me put it right.
dont_say: "Please calm down, sir." — it tells an angry person their feeling is wrong and usually makes them angrier.
positive_swap: Instead of "Calm down, there's no need to shout", say "I can hear how frustrating this has been. Let me find out what went wrong."
alt_1: Three days without internet is a long time, and I'd be annoyed too. Let me look at your line right now.
alt_2: I completely understand why you're upset. Let me see exactly what has happened and fix it.
more_formal: I fully appreciate how frustrating this has been, and your concern is entirely justified. Allow me to investigate and resolve it for you.
ask_next: So I can fix this properly, could you tell me when the connection first stopped working?
replies_you_may_hear: You're the third person I've told this to. · I don't want sympathy, I want it fixed. · Fine. What are you going to do?
if_customer_pushes: Customer: "I don't need you to understand, I need it fixed!" → Agent: "You're right. I'm checking your line now, and I'll tell you exactly what I find in two minutes."
cultural_note: Some agents answer an angry caller with "Sir, please listen to me first" or "Sir, sir, sir". International callers may hear this as talking over them; a short, specific acknowledgement before any explanation works better.
channel_note: Chat: "I can see why this is frustrating. Let me check your line now." Email: name the problem specifically in the first line, then list what you have already done.
when_not_to_use: Do not use it when the caller is calm and asking a small question; strong empathy for a small issue sounds fake.
mini_conversation: Customer: Three days! Three days without internet, and nobody does anything! | Agent: I can hear how frustrating this has been, and you have every right to be upset. Let me find out what went wrong and put it right. | Customer: Fine, but hurry. | Agent: I'm running a line test now; it takes about a minute.
source_rule: Empathy line, then action; never say "calm down", name the feeling instead.

fill_answer: right

mcq_options: A) Please calm down, sir. Shouting will not help me fix your problem any faster. | B) I can hear how frustrating this has been. Let me find out what went wrong. | C) I'm sorry for the inconvenience, sir. Many customers are facing this problem. | D) I understand, sir. Our engineers are very busy this week, so repairs are slow.
mcq_answer: B
mcq_why: B names the feeling and moves to action. A tells the caller their anger is wrong, C is a script that treats the problem as ordinary, and D is an excuse that offers no help.
reorder_task: what / find / Let / went / out / wrong / me
reorder_answer: Let me find out what went wrong
hindi_to_english: Teen din se internet nahin hai, to aapka naraaz hona bilkul jaayaz hai. Main abhi aapki line check karta/karti hoon.
hindi_to_english_answer: You've had no internet for three days, so you have every right to be upset. I'll check your line right now.
whats_next_customer: You people always say that and nothing happens.

intonation: Warm and unhurried, falling tone on "listening". Leave a clear pause after the first sentence so the caller can start again; keep the second sentence soft.
indian_pron_tip: "Listening" has a silent t: LIS-ning, not "lis-ten-ing" with a hard t. In "everything" and "anything" put the tongue tip between the teeth for th; avoid "ev-ry-ting".
register: Friendly

hindi_meaning: आप बोलते रहिए, मैं सुन रहा/रही हूँ। कोई सुझाव देने से पहले मैं पूरी बात सुनना चाहता/चाहती हूँ।
hindi_roman: Aap bolte rahiye, main sun raha/rahi hoon. Koi sujhaav dene se pehle main poori baat sunna chahta/chahti hoon.
simple_meaning: Keep talking; I am paying attention. I want the full story before I give you an answer.
why_it_works: It hands control back to the caller and promises you will not jump in with a quick fix. Customers who feel fully heard usually argue less and give you the details you need.
key_chunk: Please go on, I'm listening. I'd like to ___ before I ___.
grammar_pattern: Please + go on · I'd like to + base verb + before + I + present verb
grammar_note: After "before", use the present tense ("before I suggest") even when you mean the future.
tone_note: It sounds patient because it asks for more. Cutting in with "Yes, yes, I know, sir" sounds as if you stopped listening.
watch_out: ✗ Yes yes, I am getting it, tell fast. → ✓ Please go on, I'm listening.
dont_say: "Okay, okay, I got it." while the caller is mid-sentence — it tells them you have stopped listening and want to move on.
positive_swap: Instead of "Yes, yes, I know, let me tell you the solution", say "Please go on, I'm listening. I'd like to hear everything first."
alt_1: Take your time, I'm with you. Tell me the rest.
alt_2: No, please carry on. I want the full picture before I check anything.
more_formal: Please continue; I'd like to understand the full situation before I propose a solution.
ask_next: Thank you. So you moved in March, the bills kept going to your old address, and now there's a late fee. Have I got that right?
replies_you_may_hear: Okay, so then in April… · That's basically it. · Sorry, it's a long story.
if_customer_pushes: Customer: "Why aren't you saying anything? Are you even there?" → Agent: "Yes, I'm here and I'm taking notes. Please go on; I don't want to miss anything."
cultural_note: Some agents fill every pause with "yes sir, yes sir" or jump in with a solution to show speed. International callers often read this as impatience; short signals like "I see" and "Mm-hm" are enough.
channel_note: Chat: "Please go on, I'm reading every message." Email: not needed; instead, answer every point the customer raised.
when_not_to_use: Do not use it when the caller has clearly finished and is waiting for your answer; then you must summarise and act.
mini_conversation: Customer: So I told you I'd moved, but the bills kept going to my old flat, and then… sorry, I'll let you talk. | Agent: Please go on, I'm listening. I'd like to hear everything before I suggest anything. | Customer: Then this month I got a late fee for a bill I never saw. | Agent: I see. So the bills went to your old address, and now there's a late fee on top. Is that right?
source_rule: Interrupting frustrates customers: let them finish, take notes, then paraphrase.

I'd like to hear everything first. | C) That's fine, but could you give me only the main points, please? | D) Don't worry, sir. This is a common problem and the solution is simple.

reorder_answer: Please go on, I'm listening

sentence: I'm afraid I'm not able to refund this order, as the thirty-day return period ended last week. What I can do is give you store credit for the full amount.

linking: afraid_I'm → "a-FRAY-dime" · not_able → "no-TAY-bul" · full_amount → "fu-la-MOUNT" · What_I → "wha-dai"
intonation: A soft, slightly lower tone on "I'm afraid", then a steady, falling tone on "last week" so the reason sounds final, not over-apologetic. Short pause, then a brighter, slightly higher start on "What I can do" to signal the good news.
indian_pron_tip: "Refund" as a verb is stressed re-FUND; the noun is RE-fund. Many speakers use RE-fund for both. Also say the "-ed" in "ended" as a full extra syllable: EN-did.
register: Neutral
tone: Firm but helpful
importance: 5
customer_emotion: Disappointed and ready to argue that they deserve their money back.
memory_hook: A closed door with a lit window beside it: you point at the door only once, then spend your words on the window.
hindi_meaning: मुझे अफ़सोस है, मैं इस ऑर्डर का रिफ़ंड नहीं कर सकता/सकती, क्योंकि रिटर्न की तीस दिन की अवधि पिछले हफ़्ते ख़त्म हो गई। लेकिन मैं आपको पूरी रकम का स्टोर क्रेडिट दे सकता/सकती हूँ।

simple_meaning: I'm sorry, but I cannot give your money back because the time limit for returns is over. I can give you the full value to spend with us.
why_it_works: It gives the real reason in plain words, so the refusal does not sound random, and then moves straight to something the customer can actually have. The customer leaves with an option, not just a "no".
key_chunk: I'm afraid I'm not able to ___, as ___. What I can do is ___.
grammar_pattern: I'm afraid + I'm not able to + base verb · as + reason clause · What I can do is + base verb
grammar_note: "I'm not able to" is softer than "I can't", and "as" gives the reason, like "because".
tone_note: "I'm afraid" shows regret before the bad news, the reason makes it fair, and "What I can do" makes it helpful. "That's not possible, it's company policy" sounds like a wall and invites an argument.
watch_out: ✗ I am not able to refund this order because return period is finished last week. → ✓ I'm afraid I'm not able to refund this order, as the return period ended last week.
dont_say: "Sorry, as per policy, no refund." — it hides behind a rule, gives no reason and offers nothing.
positive_swap: Instead of "We can't refund it, it's policy", say "What I can do is give you store credit for the full amount."
alt_1: Refunds are available for thirty days after delivery, so I can't refund this one, but I can offer you full store credit today.
alt_2: The refund window closed last week, unfortunately. The good news is I can add the full amount to your account as store credit.
more_formal: I regret that a refund is no longer available, as the thirty-day return period has ended. However, I would be glad to issue store credit for the full value.
ask_next: Would you like me to add the store credit to your account now, or send you a credit code by email?

if_customer_pushes: Customer: "That's unfair, I was away for work!" → Agent: "I understand, and I'm sorry the timing worked out badly. Let me ask my team leader whether we can make an exception; if not, the store credit is still available."
cultural_note: Many agents say "As per policy" to sound official. International customers may not expect it and often hear it as hiding behind rules; a plain reason plus an alternative sounds more honest.
channel_note: Chat: split it into two messages, the reason first, then "What I can do is…". Email: reason, empathy, alternative, then a clear next step.
when_not_to_use: Do not use it when a refund might still be possible through an exception you have not checked; check first, then refuse.
mini_conversation: Customer: I want a refund for these shoes. | Agent: I'm afraid I'm not able to refund this order, as the thirty-day return period ended last week. What I can do is give you store credit for the full amount. | Customer: Hmm, can I use it on anything? | Agent: Yes, on anything on our site, and I'll email you the credit code now.
source_rule: Don't hide behind policy: give the reason in plain words, then an alternative (derived advice).

fill_answer: do

Is there anything else I can help you with? | C) I'm afraid the return period has ended, so I can't refund it, but I can offer store credit. | D) I will do my best to get you a refund. Please give me a few days to arrange it.
mcq_answer: C
mcq_why: C gives a reason, shows regret and offers an alternative. D sounds kind but promises something you cannot deliver, A hides behind policy and offers nothing, and B is a bare no.

reorder_answer: What I can do is give you store credit
