linking: if_I've → "i-fyv" · stopped_working → "stopt-WUR-king" · last_night's → "las-NIGHTS" · is_that → "iz-that"
intonation: Short pause after "So," and after "correctly". Even, falling tone through the summary, then a clear rise on "Is that right?" because it is a real yes/no question. Medium pace.
indian_pron_tip: "Stopped" is one syllable, "stopt", ending in a clear /t/; do not say "stop-ped". "Working" starts with rounded lips for /w/, with no teeth on the lip as in "v".
register: Neutral
tone: Careful, attentive check
importance: 5
customer_emotion: Annoyed, and hoping this agent has actually listened.
memory_hook: Think of a mirror: you hold the customer's own story up to them, and they nod or correct it before you touch a single wire.
hindi_meaning: तो अगर मैंने सही समझा है, तो कल रात के अपडेट के बाद से आपका इंटरनेट काम नहीं कर रहा है। क्या यह सही है?
hindi_roman: To agar maine sahi samjha hai, to kal raat ke update ke baad se aapka internet kaam nahin kar raha hai. Kya yeh sahi hai?
simple_meaning: Let me repeat your problem in my own words: your internet broke after the update. Did I get that right?
why_it_works: It proves you listened and gives the caller a chance to correct you before you start fixing the wrong thing, which saves time for both of you.
key_chunk: So, if I've understood correctly, ___. Is that right?
grammar_pattern: So, if + I've + past participle + adverb, + summary of the problem · Is that right?
grammar_note: "If I've understood" uses the present perfect because your understanding matters right now.
tone_note: It sounds careful and humble, as if any mistake would be yours. A flat "So you're saying the internet just died?" can sound as if you doubt the customer.
watch_out: ✗ If I understand correct, your internet is stopped after update. → ✓ If I've understood correctly, your internet stopped working after last night's update.
dont_say: "Okay, okay, I got it." It cuts the caller off and gives them no proof that you understood anything.
positive_swap: Instead of "What is your problem exactly?", say "So, if I've understood correctly, your internet stopped working after the update. Is that right?"
alt_1: So the internet dropped after last night's update, and it hasn't come back since. Have I got that right?
alt_2: Just to check I understand, the connection has been down since the update last night. Is that correct?
more_formal: To make sure I have understood you correctly, your internet service has not worked since last night's update. Is that accurate?
ask_next: Thanks for confirming. Have you restarted the router since then?
replies_you_may_hear: Yes, exactly. · Not quite, it was already slow before the update. · Yes, and the TV box isn't working either.
if_customer_pushes: Customer: "I just told you all that!" → Agent: "You did, and thank you. I just want to be sure of the details, because the fix depends on exactly when it stopped."
cultural_note: Many Indian agents end statements with "no?" or "isn't it?" as a check. International callers may not expect this; a clear "Is that right?" with a rising tone sounds more natural to them.
channel_note: Chat: "Just to check: your internet stopped after last night's update. Is that right?" Email: summarise the issue in the first line of your reply.
when_not_to_use: Do not paraphrase after every sentence of a long story; wait until the caller has finished, then sum up once.
mini_conversation: Customer: My internet hasn't worked since the update last night. | Agent: So, if I've understood correctly, your internet stopped working after last night's update. Is that right? | Customer: Yes, exactly. | Agent: Thanks for confirming. Let's start by checking the lights on your router.
source_rule: Paraphrase before solving ("If I've understood correctly, … Is that right?") so the customer can correct you early.

speaker: Customer
situation: A customer has noticed a monthly subscription charge on their card and calls to dispute it.
recall_cue: A caller says they never agreed to a subscription they are paying for. What might really have happened, and how do you start?
stress: i-NEV-er-SIGNED-UP · for-THIS
linking: never_signed → "NEV-er-SYND" · signed_up → "syn-DUP" · up_for → "up-fer"
intonation: Heavy stress on "never" and often on "this", with a sharp fall. It can sound accusing; the customer may feel tricked.
indian_pron_tip: When you repeat it, say "signed" as "synd", one syllable with a soft final /d/; the "g" is silent. "Never" needs a /v/ with teeth on the lip, not "newer".
register: Neutral
tone: Suspicious, accusing denial
importance: 5
customer_emotion: Feeling cheated, suspicious and worried about more charges.
memory_hook: Picture finding a gym card in your wallet that you never asked for: you want to know how it got there, and you want it gone.

hindi_roman: Maine iske liye kabhi sign up nahin kiya.
simple_meaning: I did not agree to join this service, so I should not be paying for it.
why_it_works: The customer feels tricked and fears more money will go. They need you to take it seriously, find out how it started without blaming them, and stop or refund it.
key_chunk: I never ___ for this.
grammar_pattern: I + never + past simple verb + up + for + noun
grammar_note: "Sign up for" is a phrasal verb meaning "join" or "register for".
tone_note: The customer may sound accusing. Saying "You must have clicked on it" blames them and turns the call into an argument.
watch_out: ✗ (Agent) You must have done it by mistake. → ✓ I understand. Let's find out how this started, and I'll stop it if it shouldn't be there.
dont_say: "You must have subscribed yourself." It blames the customer before you have checked anything.
positive_swap: Instead of "It shows you subscribed", say "Let's find out how this started. I can see it began on 3 May. Does that date mean anything to you?"
alt_1: I didn't agree to this at all.
alt_2: I don't remember joining anything like this.
more_formal: I have no record of subscribing to this service.
ask_next: I understand, and I'll look into it now. I can see it started on 3 May after a free trial. Does that ring a bell?
replies_you_may_hear: No, never. · Oh, maybe that free trial? · Just cancel it and give me my money back.
if_customer_pushes: Customer: "I want all my money back!" → Agent: "I understand. I'll cancel it now so nothing more is taken, and I'll check what I can refund for the past months."
cultural_note: Indian agents may say "as per our records, you have subscribed". International customers may not expect that phrase and may hear it as "you are lying"; describing what you see and asking is calmer.
channel_note: Chat: often "i never signed up for this??" or "didnt sign up for anything". Email: may include the bank statement; quote the date and amount back to them.
when_not_to_use: Do not say "I can see you signed up" as your first line, even if it is true; it sounds like an argument before you have listened.
mini_conversation: Customer: I never signed up for this. | Agent: I understand, and I'll look into it now. I can see it started on 3 May after a free trial. Does that ring a bell? | Customer: Oh, maybe. But I don't want it. | Agent: Of course. I'll cancel it now, so there are no more charges.
source_rule: Don't hide behind records or policy: listen without blaming, give the facts in plain words, then offer what you can do.

kind: Understand it

sentence: I'll think about it and get back to you.
speaker: Customer
situation: You have offered the customer an upgrade to a faster broadband plan, and they answer at the end of the call.
recall_cue: At the end of an offer, a caller says they will think about it. What might they really mean, and how do you close well?
stress: i'll-THINK-a-BOUT-it · and-GET-BACK-to-you
linking: think_about_it → "thing-kuh-BOU-tit" · get_back → "geh-BACK" · back_to_you → "BAK-tuh-yoo"
intonation: A light, falling tone often means a polite "no for now". A slower, thoughtful tone means they are really considering it. Listen to the pace, not only the words.
indian_pron_tip: When you reply, say "think" with /θ/, tongue between the teeth, not "tink". In "about it", link the /t/ into "it": "a-bou-tit", not two separate words.
register: Neutral
tone: Polite, non-committal
importance: 4
customer_emotion: Unsure, slightly pressured, wanting space to decide.
memory_hook: Picture a shopper putting a shirt back on the rail and saying "maybe later": push and they walk out; hand them the tag and they may come back.
hindi_meaning: मैं इसके बारे में सोचूँगा/सोचूँगी और फिर आपको बताऊँगा/बताऊँगी।
hindi_roman: Main iske baare mein sochoonga/sochoongi aur phir aapko bataoonga/bataoongi.
simple_meaning: I am not ready to decide now; maybe I will contact you later.
why_it_works: It is often a polite way to say "not now" without saying no. The customer wants no pressure; making it easy to decide later keeps their trust and the chance of a yes.
key_chunk: I'll think about it and ___.
grammar_pattern: I'll + think about + it + and + get back to + person
grammar_note: "Get back to you" means contact you again later; it is the common international form.
tone_note: The customer is polite but closing the topic. Pushing with "But this offer ends today!" sounds pushy and can damage trust.
watch_out: ✗ (Agent) Okay, then you call back to us. → ✓ Of course, take your time. Shall I email you the details so you can compare?
dont_say: "But sir, this is the best offer, you should take it now." It ignores the customer's answer and feels like pressure.
positive_swap: Instead of "Why don't you decide now?", say "Of course, take your time. Shall I email you the details so you can compare?"
alt_1: Let me think it over and I'll let you know.
alt_2: I'm not sure yet. I'll call you back if I decide to go ahead.
more_formal: I would like some time to consider it, and I will contact you once I have decided.
ask_next: Of course, take your time. Would it help if I emailed you the details so you can compare?
replies_you_may_hear: Yes, please email it. · No, I'll look on the website. · Okay, but I probably won't change.
if_customer_pushes: Customer: "I said I'll think about it!" → Agent: "Absolutely, no pressure at all. The details will be in your email whenever you're ready."
cultural_note: Indian customers often say "I will let you know" or "Let me discuss with family and revert". International customers more often say "I'll get back to you" or "Let me think it over". All of them usually mean "not yet", so answer them the same way: no pressure.
channel_note: Chat: often "ok will think n let u know". Email: "I'll consider it and come back to you." Reply with a short, no-pressure summary.
when_not_to_use: Do not offer to call them back about it unless they agree; an unwanted sales call feels like pressure.
mini_conversation: Customer: I'll think about it and get back to you. | Agent: Of course, take your time. Would it help if I emailed you the details so you can compare? | Customer: Yes, please. | Agent: Done. It's in your inbox now. Is there anything else I can help you with today?
source_rule: Respect the customer's decision: avoid pressure, and leave one clear, easy next step.
